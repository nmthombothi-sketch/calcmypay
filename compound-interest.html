The mechanics behind compounding, explained simply

Compound interest is often described as "interest on interest" — a phrase that is accurate but abstract. In practical terms, it means every rand you invest starts earning its own returns, and those returns then earn returns too. The effect is small in year one and dramatic by year twenty, which is exactly why time in the market matters more than almost any other single factor in building wealth.

A concrete illustration of the acceleration

Consider R100,000 invested at a steady 10% annual return, with no further contributions. In year one, you earn R10,000. But by year ten, you are earning roughly R23,600 in that single year alone — more than double the original annual gain — simply because the base you are earning on has grown.

Why monthly contributions change the outcome so dramatically

A lump sum compounds on itself, but regular monthly contributions add fresh capital that then also has years to compound. This is why the calculator above weights consistent monthly investing so heavily — someone contributing R2,000/month for 20 years typically ends up with a meaningfully larger balance than someone who invests a much larger lump sum once and stops.

The real-world variables this calculator simplifies

  • Returns are never constant — markets move up and down year to year; this tool assumes a smoothed average for clarity, not a guarantee
  • Inflation erodes real value — a rand in 20 years buys less than a rand today, so your real (inflation-adjusted) growth is lower than the nominal figure shown
  • Fees matter more than people expect — a 2% annual fee versus a 0.5% fee compounds into a very large difference over decades
  • Tax treatment varies — growth inside a tax-free savings account compounds untaxed, while growth in a regular account is taxed along the way

Where compounding works hardest for South Africans

A tax-free savings account is one of the most direct ways to let compounding work without the drag of tax on your growth. Combined with a retirement annuity — which adds an upfront tax deduction on top of tax-free growth — these two vehicles are the backbone of most disciplined long-term investing strategies in South Africa.

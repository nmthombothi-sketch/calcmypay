The gap between gross and net that surprises new employees

Starting a first job or negotiating a new salary, it is easy to anchor on the gross figure quoted in an offer letter. The real number that determines your lifestyle — what actually lands in your account — is often 20-30% lower once PAYE, UIF and any deductions are accounted for. Understanding this gap before accepting an offer prevents an unpleasant surprise on your first payslip.

Why this matters most at salary negotiation time

When comparing two job offers, always compare like for like — either both in gross terms or both in net terms, never one of each. A company quoting a slightly lower gross salary but including strong benefits (medical aid contribution, pension matching) can genuinely leave you better off than a higher gross offer with no benefits, once the full picture is calculated.

Why the gap between gross and net surprises so many people

A job offer quoting R30,000 "gross" and R30,000 "net" imply very different actual take-home amounts, yet the distinction is often glossed over in casual conversation about salary. Gross is your salary before any deductions; net is what actually reaches your bank account after PAYE, UIF, and any other deductions like retirement contributions or medical aid.

The deductions that create the gap

DeductionWhat it isTypical impact
PAYE (income tax)Progressive tax based on SARS bracketsLargest deduction for most earners
UIF1% of salary, capped annuallySmall but consistent
Retirement fund contributionsPension or provident fund, if applicableVaries by employer scheme
Medical aidIf deducted at sourceVaries by plan and dependents

Why the percentage gap grows as income rises

South Africa uses a progressive tax system, meaning higher income is taxed at higher marginal rates. This means the gap between gross and net widens, proportionally, as salary increases — someone earning R15,000/month might see a gap of around 10-12%, while someone earning R80,000/month can see a gap closer to 30% once medical credits and rebates are properly accounted for.

Where this calculation matters most in practice

  • Comparing job offers: always convert to net before comparing, since gross figures alone can be misleading if benefits or structures differ
  • Salary negotiations: understanding your own gap helps you negotiate the right gross figure to achieve a target take-home
  • Budgeting for a new role: plan your monthly budget around net, not gross, to avoid an unpleasant surprise on your first payslip

Building this into a fuller financial picture

Knowing your net income is the starting point for every other financial decision — what you can afford, how much you can save, and what your tax position looks like.

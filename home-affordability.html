Beyond the bond: what buying a home really costs

The monthly bond repayment this calculator estimates is the headline number, but it is far from the only cost of buying property in South Africa. A realistic affordability picture accounts for the upfront transaction costs and the ongoing costs of ownership that continue long after the sale is finalised.

Upfront costs: transfer duty and bond registration

Transfer duty is a tax paid to SARS on property purchases, calculated on a sliding scale. Properties under R1.1 million currently attract no transfer duty, but above that threshold, the tax increases progressively. On top of transfer duty, you will pay conveyancing (transfer) attorney fees and bond registration attorney fees — together these upfront legal and tax costs typically add up to 8-10% of the purchase price.

Purchase price Approx. transfer duty Transfer attorney Bond registration Total upfront
R900,000 R0 R25,000 R20,000 R70,000 - R90,000
R1,500,000 R12,000 R35,000 R28,000 R135,000 - R165,000
R2,500,000 R56,000 R50,000 R40,000 R225,000 - R275,000
R4,000,000 R160,000 R70,000 R55,000 R380,000 - R450,000

Critical insight: You need cash available for these upfront costs in addition to your deposit. On a R2.5 million home with a 10% deposit (R250,000), you still need another R250,000+ for transfer and bond costs. This "hidden" requirement catches many first-time buyers off guard.

Ongoing costs that continue for as long as you own

Your bond repayment is just the beginning. Monthly ongoing costs typically add 30-50% to your base bond repayment:

Ongoing Cost Typical Range Notes
Rates & taxes R800 - R4,000/month 0.5-1% of property value annually
Levies (sectional title) R1,500 - R5,000/month Complex/townhouse maintenance
Levies (security estate) R3,000 - R15,000/month Includes security, amenities
Homeowner's insurance R500 - R2,000/month Often required by bank
Maintenance (1% rule) 1% of value annually R2,000/month on R2.4m home
Utilities R2,000 - R5,000/month Electricity, water, refuse
Security (if applicable) R500 - R2,000/month Armed response, alarms

Worked example: True cost of a R2 million home

Cost Category Monthly Amount Annual Amount
Bond repayment (11%, 20yr, 10% deposit) R18,500 R222,000
Rates & taxes R2,000 R24,000
Homeowner's insurance R800 R9,600

Key insight: The true monthly cost is R25,970, not just the R18,500 bond repayment. Budgeting only for the bond creates a R7,470/month shortfall that forces compromises elsewhere or credit card debt.

Why a bigger deposit changes more than your bond amount

A larger deposit does more than reduce what you owe — it can also improve the interest rate your bank offers, since a lower loan-to-value (LTV) ratio represents less risk to the lender. A 20% deposit instead of 10% can meaningfully lower both your monthly repayment and your total interest paid over the life of the bond.

Impact of deposit size on R2 million home (20 years at 11%)

Deposit Loan Amount Monthly Repayment Total Interest Typical Rate
0% (100% bond) R2,000,000 R20,550 R2,932,000 11.5% (prime+1)
10% (R200,000) R1,800,000 R18,500 R2,640,000 11% (prime)
20% (R400,000) R1,600,000 R16,200 R2,288,000 10.5% (prime-0.5)
30% (R600,000) R1,400,000 R14,000 R1,960,000 10% (prime-1)

Result: A R400,000 deposit (20%) saves R644,000 in interest versus a 100% bond and reduces monthly repayments by R4,350. Every R100,000 of deposit saves approximately R150,000-R200,000 in total interest.

FLISP: Government subsidy for first-time buyers

The Finance Linked Individual Subsidy Programme (FLISP) is a government initiative administered through the National Housing Finance Corporation (NHFC) to help qualifying first-time buyers afford their first home.

Who qualifies for FLISP

  • First-time home buyer: Never owned residential property before
  • SA citizen or permanent resident: Must have valid ID
  • Income between R3,501 and R22,000/month: Gross household income
  • Buying primary residence: Not investment property
  • Property price limits: Generally under R300,000 for subsidised homes
  • Dependents or married: Single applicants over 30 may also qualify

FLISP subsidy amounts by income (2026)

Monthly Income Maximum Subsidy Best Use
R3,501 - R5,500 R130,505 Deposit or reduce bond
R5,501 - R7,000 R120,354 Deposit or reduce bond
R7,001 - R8,000 R110,201 Deposit or reduce bond
R8,001 - R10,000 R90,000 Deposit or reduce bond
R10,001 - R15,000 R70,000 Deposit or reduce bond
R15,001 - R22,000 R30,000 Deposit or reduce bond

How to apply: Apply through your bank when applying for a bond, or directly through NHFC. The subsidy is paid directly into the bond or to cover transfer costs. Processing takes 2-3 months, so apply early in your buying process.

FLISP is often overlooked: Many first-time buyers earning between R10,000-R22,000/month don't realise they qualify for a R30,000-R90,000 subsidy that can meaningfully reduce their deposit requirement or bond amount. Always check eligibility before buying.

20-year vs 30-year bonds: the true cost difference

The choice between 20-year and 30-year bonds involves a significant trade-off between monthly affordability and total cost.

Comparison: R1.5 million bond at 11%

Term Monthly Repayment Total Paid Total Interest
15 years R17,100 R3,078,000 R1,578,000
20 years R15,400 R3,696,000 R2,196,000
25 years R14,500 R4,350,000 R2,850,000
30 years R14,000 R5,040,000 R3,540,000

Key insight: A 30-year bond saves only R1,400/month compared to 20 years but costs R1.34 million MORE in total interest. If you can afford the 20-year payment, choose it. If you need the 30-year payment for affordability, commit to paying extra when possible.

Interest rate sensitivity: why rate environment matters enormously

Interest rate dramatically affects affordability. The same monthly payment supports very different bond amounts at different rates:

Bond amount supported by R10,000/month repayment (20-year term)

Interest Rate Max Bond Total Interest Context
8% (prime -2.5) R1,230,000 R1,170,000 Exceptional credit, low-rate environment
10% (prime -0.5) R1,070,000 R1,330,000 Good credit, favourable rates
11% (prime) R1,000,000 R1,400,000 Standard rates 2026
12% (prime +1) R930,000 R1,470,000 Average credit or higher rates
14% (prime +3) R810,000 R1,590,000 Poor credit or high-rate environment

Impact: Moving from 11% to 14% interest reduces your affordable bond by R190,000 (19%) on the same monthly payment. Each 1% rate increase reduces affordability by approximately 8-10%. This is why your credit score and the rate environment matter so much.

The rent-versus-buy decision this calculator does not make for you

Affording a bond and it being the right financial decision right now are two different questions. Several factors influence whether buying or renting makes more sense:

Buying makes sense when:

  • You plan to stay 7+ years (allows equity building to offset transaction costs)
  • Property prices in your area are reasonable relative to rents
  • You have stable income and can handle unexpected repairs
  • You value stability and freedom to modify your home
  • You have emergency savings to handle surprises

Renting makes sense when:

  • You might move within 5 years (transaction costs eat returns)
  • You need flexibility for career changes
  • Property prices in your area are inflated vs rents
  • You prefer predictable monthly costs without maintenance surprises
  • You want to invest surplus money elsewhere for better returns

If you're unsure whether buying makes sense for your timeline and situation, our rent vs buy guide walks through the trade-offs in detail, and the rent affordability calculator lets you compare the two paths on the same terms.

The approval process: what banks actually look at

While this calculator uses the 30% rule of thumb, banks consider many factors when approving bonds:

Primary approval factors

  • Credit score: 670+ generally needed for prime rates, 600+ for approval
  • Employment stability: 2+ years with current employer preferred
  • Income verification: Payslips, bank statements, tax returns
  • Debt-to-income ratio: Total debt (including bond) under 36% of income
  • Living expenses: Banks use NCA-prescribed expense ratios
  • Deposit size: Larger deposits improve approval chances
  • Property valuation: Bank's valuer must agree with purchase price

Improving your approval chances

  • Check your credit report: Free from TransUnion, Experian, XDS annually
  • Reduce existing debt: Lower debt-to-income ratio
  • Save larger deposit: 20%+ dramatically improves approval
  • Use a bond originator: BetterBond, Ooba apply to multiple banks simultaneously
  • Get pre-approval: Know your budget before house-hunting
  • Stabilise employment: Avoid job changes before applying

Hidden costs that catch buyers off guard

Beyond the calculated costs, several less-obvious expenses affect homeowners:

  • Moving costs: R5,000-R20,000 depending on distance and volume
  • Immediate repairs/renovations: Most homes need R20,000-R100,000 in updates
  • Furniture and appliances: R50,000-R200,000 for a new home
  • Connection fees: Internet, security system, DStv installation
  • Garden establishment: R10,000-R50,000 for landscaping
  • Special levies: Sectional title schemes occasionally levy R10,000-R50,000 for major repairs
  • Property value declines: Markets can drop 10-20% in downturns

Budget recommendation: Set aside 5-10% of purchase price for immediate costs beyond transfer fees. On a R2 million home, that's R100,000-R200,000 in addition to the R200,000+ transfer costs.

Common affordability mistakes South African buyers make

  • Budgeting only for the bond: Forgetting rates, levies, insurance, maintenance adds 30-50%
  • Using the maximum bank approval: Just because they approve R2.5m doesn't mean you should spend R2.5m
  • Ignoring upfront costs: Not having cash for transfer costs (8-10%) beyond deposit
  • Choosing 30-year terms without a plan: Paying R1.34 million more in interest vs 20 years
  • Not checking FLISP eligibility: Missing R30,000-R130,000 in first-time buyer subsidies
  • Ignoring interest rate risk: Budgeting at current rates without 2-3% buffer for increases
  • Skipping pre-approval: Falling in love with homes you can't actually afford
  • Not budgeting for lifestyle: Housing over 35% of income leaves no room for living
  • Estate levies surprise: R10,000/month levies on R2m homes shock many buyers
  • Maintenance underestimation: Older homes need 2-3% annually, not 1%

Your home-buying action plan

  1. Check your credit score: Address any issues 6+ months before applying
  2. Calculate your true budget: Use this calculator with 25% ratio for breathing room
  3. Save deposit + upfront costs: Target 10-20% deposit plus 10% for transfer costs
  4. Check FLISP eligibility: If first-time buyer, apply early in process
  5. Get pre-approved: Know your exact budget before house hunting
  6. Budget total cost: Include all ongoing costs, not just the bond
  7. Build emergency fund: 3-6 months expenses including housing costs
  8. Compare rent vs buy: Ensure buying makes sense for your timeline
  9. Use bond originator: Apply to multiple banks for best rate
  10. Choose term wisely: 20 years if affordable, 30 years with extra payment plan

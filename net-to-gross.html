Tips / Salary Negotiation in SA

Always negotiate on gross, not net

When discussing salary with an employer, always use gross figures. Net pay varies depending on your tax situation, deductions and benefits. Gross salary is the objective number both parties can agree on.

Clarify CTC vs basic salary

Ask whether the offered figure is CTC (total cost to company including benefits) or basic salary. A R40,000 CTC offer may only result in R28,000 cash take-home once medical aid, pension and other costs are included.

When working backwards from net pay actually matters

Most salary conversations start from gross, but there are specific, common situations where you need to work in the opposite direction — you know what you need to take home each month, and you need to figure out what gross salary or rate would get you there. This is a fundamentally different, more useful calculation for these situations than a standard forward calculation.

The situations where this reverse calculation is essential

  • Negotiating a new role — you know your target take-home from budgeting your life, and need to know what gross figure to ask for
  • Freelance rate setting — working out what to charge to hit a specific personal income target after tax
  • Relocation planning — figuring out what salary you would need in a new city to maintain your current lifestyle
  • Comparing a contract role to permanent employment — contractors often think in net terms, while permanent roles are advertised in gross

Why this calculation is not simply "reverse the percentage"

Because South Africa uses a progressive tax system with rebates and thresholds, you cannot simply divide your target net by a fixed percentage to find the gross figure — the effective tax rate itself changes depending on the income level, meaning the calculation has to work iteratively through the actual tax brackets to land on an accurate gross figure.

A worked example

If your target take-home is R25,000/month, a naive "add back 20%" estimate might suggest R30,000 gross — but the actual required gross salary, once PAYE, UIF, and rebates are properly calculated, typically comes out meaningfully different, which is exactly why using an accurate calculator rather than a rough mental estimate matters for anything beyond a ballpark figure.

Using this figure in a negotiation

Knowing your required gross figure gives you a concrete, defensible number to anchor a salary negotiation around, rather than a vague sense of "I need more." It also lets you evaluate an offer instantly — if a prospective employer proposes a gross figure below your calculated requirement, you know immediately it will not meet your actual take-home needs.

Putting the resulting number to work

Once you have your target gross figure, use the salary calculator to double-check the full breakdown including medical aid and retirement contributions, or the salary comparison tool if you are weighing this figure against a competing offer.

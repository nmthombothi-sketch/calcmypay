The real cost of a car goes well beyond the repayment

A vehicle is often the second-largest purchase most South Africans make, yet it is frequently budgeted using only the monthly instalment — ignoring insurance, fuel, maintenance, and depreciation. This calculator focuses on what you can sensibly finance, but understanding the total cost of ownership is what actually protects your budget.

The full monthly cost breakdown

On a typical mid-range vehicle financed at R4,500/month, the true all-in monthly cost usually looks closer to this:

Cost itemTypical monthly amount
Loan repaymentR4,500
Comprehensive insuranceR900 - R1,800
Fuel (average commute)R1,800 - R3,000
Maintenance and services (averaged)R500 - R900
Licensing (annualised)R50 - R100
Total realistic monthly costR7,750 - R10,300

Why the loan term matters more than the monthly figure

A longer loan term reduces your monthly instalment, which can make an unaffordable car look affordable on paper. But longer terms mean paying significantly more in total interest, and you spend more years owing more than the car is worth — a position called being "upside down" on the loan, which becomes a real problem if you need to sell or trade in early.

The balloon payment trap

Balloon or residual payments reduce your monthly instalment by deferring a large lump sum to the end of the loan. They can make sense for those who plan to trade in before the balloon is due, but they are frequently used to make an otherwise unaffordable car seem manageable. If you cannot comfortably afford the car without a balloon, the balloon itself is a signal to consider a cheaper vehicle.

New versus used: the depreciation math

A new car loses roughly 20-30% of its value in the first two to three years — the steepest part of its depreciation curve. Buying a well-maintained vehicle that is two to three years old means someone else has already absorbed that initial loss, often for a similar vehicle at a meaningfully lower price.

Matching your car to your broader financial picture

The 15% rule used in this calculator is a starting point, not a target to maximise. Every rand committed to a car payment is a rand unavailable for saving or investing — money that, left in a compounding investment instead, can grow substantially over the same period. See what that trade-off looks like concretely with our compound interest calculator, and make sure your total transport spend still fits your overall budget using the lifestyle affordability tool.

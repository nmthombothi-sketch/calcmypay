Understanding overtime pay in South Africa

Overtime pay is one of the most misunderstood aspects of South African employment law. Many workers don't know exactly what they're entitled to, how their overtime rate is calculated, or whether they even qualify for overtime protection under the law. This comprehensive guide explains everything you need to know about overtime pay under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA), including the legal minimum rates, who qualifies, and how to calculate what your extra hours are actually worth.

Whether you're regularly working overtime, occasionally called in on weekends, or trying to understand whether your employer is meeting their legal obligations, this calculator and guide will give you the clarity you need. All calculations are based on the current BCEA provisions and the earnings threshold effective from 1 May 2026.

What the BCEA actually requires for overtime

The Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA) sets the legal minimum standards for overtime compensation in South Africa. These are minimum requirements — your employment contract or sectoral determination may provide more generous terms, but no employer can legally pay less than what the BCEA requires.

The legal minimum overtime rates

South African law recognizes two different overtime rates depending on when the work is performed:

  • Weekday and Saturday overtime: At least 1.5 times your normal hourly rate. This applies to any work beyond your normal working hours on Monday through Saturday.
  • Sunday and public holiday work: At least 2 times your normal hourly rate, OR your normal rate plus a paid day off in lieu. This recognizes Sundays and public holidays as protected rest days.
Important: These are minimum rates. Many employers, particularly in unionized sectors or professional services, pay higher overtime rates as part of their employment policies or collective agreements. Always check your specific contract.

Maximum overtime hours

The BCEA generally limits overtime to 10 hours per week for covered employees. Additionally, total working time (ordinary hours plus overtime) may not exceed:

  • 12 hours on any single day
  • 45 hours per week for employees working 5 days or fewer
  • 48 hours per week for employees working 6 days

If you're regularly working significantly more than these limits, it's worth understanding whether this is compliant with your specific employment arrangement and whether you're being properly compensated.

Who qualifies for overtime protection?

Not every employee in South Africa is automatically entitled to overtime pay under the BCEA. The law excludes certain categories of workers from these specific protections:

The BCEA earnings threshold

The most important factor is whether you earn above or below the BCEA earnings threshold. As of 1 May 2026, this threshold is R269,600.90 per year (approximately R22,466.74 per month).

  • Earning below the threshold: You are entitled to the full protection of BCEA overtime provisions. Your employer must pay you at least 1.5x for weekday overtime and 2x for Sunday/public holiday work.
  • Earning above the threshold: You may be excluded from BCEA overtime protections, depending on your employment contract. Many employers still pay overtime to higher earners as a matter of policy or contractual agreement.

Senior management exclusion

Senior managerial employees — those with authority to make significant decisions about the business and their own working hours — may also be excluded from overtime provisions, regardless of their earnings level.

Sectoral determinations

Certain industries have sectoral determinations that may override or supplement the general BCEA provisions. These include:

  • Farm workers
  • Domestic workers
  • Hospitality sector
  • Security services
  • Wholesale and retail

Each sectoral determination has its own specific rules about overtime rates, maximum hours, and rest periods. If you work in one of these sectors, check the specific determination that applies to your industry.

How to calculate your overtime rate

Calculating your overtime pay requires first determining your normal hourly rate. Here's the step-by-step process:

Step 1: Determine your normal hourly rate

If you're paid monthly, convert to an hourly rate using this formula:

  • Monthly salary × 12 = Annual salary
  • Annual salary ÷ 52 weeks = Weekly salary
  • Weekly salary ÷ Normal hours per week = Hourly rate

For example, if you earn R20,000 per month and work 45 hours per week:

  • R20,000 × 12 = R240,000 per year
  • R240,000 ÷ 52 = R4,615.38 per week
  • R4,615.38 ÷ 45 = R102.56 per hour

Step 2: Apply the overtime multiplier

Once you have your normal hourly rate:

  • Weekday/Saturday overtime: Hourly rate × 1.5
  • Sunday/Public holiday: Hourly rate × 2.0
Worked Example

Calculating overtime on R20,000 monthly salary

Let's work through a realistic scenario: You earn R20,000 per month, work 45 hours per week normally, and in one week you work 8 hours of weekday overtime plus 4 hours on a Sunday.

Step 1: Calculate normal hourly rate

  • R20,000 × 12 = R240,000 per year
  • R240,000 ÷ 52 = R4,615.38 per week
  • R4,615.38 ÷ 45 = R102.56 per hour

Step 2: Calculate overtime rates

  • Weekday overtime rate: R102.56 × 1.5 = R153.84 per hour
  • Sunday rate: R102.56 × 2.0 = R205.12 per hour

Step 3: Calculate total overtime pay

  • 8 hours weekday overtime: 8 × R153.84 = R1,230.72
  • 4 hours Sunday work: 4 × R205.12 = R820.48
  • Total overtime: R2,051.20

For those 12 hours of extra work, you would earn R2,051.20 in overtime pay, in addition to your normal salary. This is before tax — overtime is added to your regular income and taxed at your marginal rate.

How overtime interacts with your tax

A common misconception is that overtime is taxed at a special lower rate, or conversely, that it's taxed at a punitive higher rate. The reality is simpler: overtime pay is added to your regular salary for the month it's earned and taxed at your marginal rate — the rate that applies to your combined income for that specific month.

Why overtime doesn't usually change your tax bracket

Because overtime is typically irregular, it doesn't usually push you into a higher annual tax bracket. Your annual tax liability is calculated on your total yearly earnings, not on any single month in isolation. However, in a month with substantial overtime, a portion of that month's income may be taxed at your marginal rate, which could be higher than your average effective rate.

Example: Overtime and marginal tax

If you earn R300,000 per year (26% marginal rate) and earn an additional R5,000 in overtime one month, that R5,000 will be taxed at approximately 26%. You would keep roughly R3,700 after tax. The overtime doesn't change the tax rate on your regular salary — it's simply added on top and taxed at your marginal rate.

Time off in lieu vs overtime payment

The BCEA allows for an alternative to cash payment for Sunday and public holiday work: you may receive your normal rate plus a paid day off in lieu. This arrangement must be agreed upon in writing and the time off must be granted within a reasonable period.

For weekday and Saturday overtime, the BCEA requires cash payment at 1.5x — time off in lieu is not a legal alternative for these hours, though some employers may offer it as an additional benefit beyond the legal minimum.

Strategies for managing overtime income

Overtime income is often irregular and unpredictable, which makes it poorly suited for baseline monthly budgeting. Here are strategic approaches to make the most of overtime earnings:

1

Direct to Emergency Fund

Because overtime is irregular, it's perfect for building your emergency fund without affecting your regular budget. Aim for 3-6 months of expenses.

2

Specific Savings Goals

Allocate overtime to specific goals like a car deposit, holiday, or home improvements. This keeps it separate from regular spending and gives you clear targets.

3

Debt Reduction

Use irregular overtime income to make extra payments on high-interest debt like credit cards or personal loans. This accelerates debt payoff without straining your monthly budget.

4

Retirement Contributions

Consider directing overtime to a retirement annuity. This reduces your taxable income (saving tax at your marginal rate) while building long-term wealth.

5

Avoid Lifestyle Creep

Don't increase your regular expenses based on overtime income. When overtime decreases (as it often does), you'll be stuck with higher fixed costs you can't afford.

6

Track and Review

Keep records of your overtime hours and payments. Review quarterly to understand patterns and ensure you're being paid correctly for all hours worked.

Record-keeping and disputes

Under the BCEA, employers must keep records of employees' working hours and overtime. However, it's wise to keep your own records as well. Track:

  • Daily hours worked (start time, end time, breaks)
  • Which days were overtime vs normal hours
  • Sunday and public holiday work
  • Any agreements about time off in lieu

If you believe you're not being paid the correct overtime rates, first raise the issue with your employer or HR department. If this doesn't resolve the matter, you can refer a dispute to the CCMA (Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration) or the Department of Employment and Labour.

Glossary: Overtime terms explained

BCEA
Basic Conditions of Employment Act — the primary legislation governing working hours, overtime, leave, and other employment conditions in South Africa.
Earnings Threshold
The annual earnings level (R269,600.90 from May 2026) above which employees may be excluded from certain BCEA protections, including overtime provisions.
Normal Hours
The regular working hours specified in your employment contract, typically 45 hours per week. Overtime is calculated as hours worked beyond this.
Time Off in Lieu
Paid time off granted instead of cash payment for overtime work. Allowed for Sunday/public holiday work under BCEA, but not for weekday overtime.
Marginal Tax Rate
The tax rate applied to your next rand of income. Overtime is taxed at this rate, which may be higher than your average effective tax rate.
Sectoral Determination
Industry-specific employment regulations that may override general BCEA provisions for certain sectors like farming, domestic work, or hospitality.

Frequently asked questions

How is overtime calculated in South Africa?

Overtime is calculated by first determining your normal hourly rate (monthly salary × 12 ÷ 52 weeks ÷ normal weekly hours), then applying the appropriate multiplier: 1.5x for weekday/Saturday overtime and 2x for Sunday/public holiday work.

What is the overtime rate in South Africa?

The minimum overtime rate is 1.5 times your normal hourly rate for weekdays and Saturdays. Work on Sundays and public holidays must be paid at 2 times your normal rate, or you may receive your normal rate plus a paid day off in lieu.

Who qualifies for overtime pay in South Africa?

Employees earning below the BCEA earnings threshold of R269,600.90 per year (effective 1 May 2026) are entitled to overtime pay. Senior managers and employees earning above this threshold may be excluded depending on their employment contract.

Is overtime taxed differently in South Africa?

No. Overtime pay is added to your regular salary for the month it's earned and taxed at your marginal rate. There is no special lower or higher tax rate for overtime income.

Can my employer refuse to pay me overtime?

If you earn below the BCEA earnings threshold (R269,600.90/year) and you've worked overtime as required or authorized by your employer, they must pay you at the legal minimum rates. Refusing to pay lawful overtime is a violation of the BCEA.

What's the maximum overtime I can work?

The BCEA generally limits overtime to 10 hours per week. Total working time (normal hours plus overtime) cannot exceed 12 hours per day or 45-48 hours per week depending on your work schedule.

Do I get overtime for working on public holidays?

Yes. Work on public holidays must be paid at 2 times your normal rate, or you may receive your normal rate plus a paid day off in lieu. This applies to employees earning below the BCEA threshold.

What if my employer offers time off instead of overtime pay?

Time off in lieu is only legally acceptable for Sunday and public holiday work, and must be agreed in writing. For weekday and Saturday overtime, the BCEA requires cash payment at 1.5x — time off in lieu is not a legal alternative.

Does overtime count towards my UIF contributions?

Yes. UIF contributions are calculated on your total remuneration, including overtime pay. However, UIF is capped at R177.12 per month (1% of R17,712), so if your regular salary already exceeds this ceiling, no additional UIF is deducted from overtime.

What should I do if I'm not being paid the correct overtime?

First raise the issue with your employer or HR department in writing. Keep records of your hours worked and what you were paid. If the matter isn't resolved, you can refer a dispute to the CCMA or contact the Department of Employment and Labour.

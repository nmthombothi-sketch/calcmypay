Your rights and rates for overtime pay under South African law

What the law actually requires

The Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA) sets the minimum standards for overtime pay in South Africa. Employers cannot legally pay less than these rates for qualifying overtime work, though many pay more as part of their own policies.

The legal minimum rates

  • Weekday and Saturday overtime — at least 1.5 times your normal hourly rate.
  • Sunday and public holiday work — at least 2 times your normal hourly rate, or a paid day off plus your normal rate, depending on your agreement.
  • Maximum overtime — generally capped at 10 hours per week, and total working hours (normal plus overtime) may not exceed 12 hours per day.

Who this applies to

The BCEA overtime provisions apply to most employees, but there are exclusions — senior managerial employees, and anyone earning above the earnings threshold set annually by the Minister of Employment and Labour, may be excluded from these specific protections, depending on their contract.

Overtime and your tax

Overtime pay is added to your normal salary for that month and taxed at your marginal rate — there is no separate, lower tax rate for overtime earnings. A big overtime month can occasionally push part of your income into a higher tax bracket for that month, though your annual tax liability is calculated on your total yearly earnings, not any single month in isolation.

The legal framework behind overtime pay in South Africa

The Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA) sets the legal minimum standards for overtime compensation, though many employment contracts and sectoral agreements provide more generous terms. Understanding the legal baseline helps you recognise whether your employer's overtime policy meets, exceeds, or potentially falls short of what the law requires.

Who is and is not covered by BCEA overtime provisions

Not every employee is entitled to overtime under the BCEA. Employees earning above a specified annual earnings threshold, and those in certain senior management or specific sectoral roles, may be excluded from these provisions. It is worth checking your specific employment contract and sector to understand which rules apply to your situation.

Why the 1.5x and 2x rates exist

Time workedRateRationale
Normal weekday/Saturday overtime1.5xCompensates for time beyond the standard work week
Sunday work2x (or a day off in lieu)Sundays are treated as a protected rest day by default
Public holidays2xRecognises the significance of public holidays as rest days

The BCEA overtime hour limits

The BCEA generally limits overtime to 10 hours per week for covered employees, and total working time (ordinary plus overtime) is capped to protect against excessive hours. If you are regularly working significantly more than this, it is worth understanding whether this is compliant with your specific employment arrangement.

How overtime interacts with your tax bracket

Overtime pay is added to your ordinary income for the month it is earned and taxed at your marginal rate — the rate applicable to your combined income for that specific month, not a special "overtime rate." This means a month with substantial overtime can push a portion of your income into a higher tax bracket for that month specifically, even if your average annual income remains in a lower bracket.

Making overtime work for your broader financial picture

Overtime income is often irregular, which makes it well suited to specific financial goals rather than baseline monthly budgeting. Consider directing overtime earnings straight into your emergency fund or a specific savings goal, rather than letting it blend into regular spending where its impact is harder to track.

Our Mission

"Help every South African understand their salary, plan their money, and build their future — with free, accurate, easy-to-use tools that work for everyone, regardless of income or financial literacy."

Why we built CalcMyPay

Too many South Africans have no clear picture of their own money. How much do you really take home after tax? What can you actually afford? Are you saving enough to retire? The answers are often locked away in confusing tax tables, behind expensive financial advisers, or scattered across dozens of websites with outdated information.

The result: people make major financial decisions — buying a home, changing jobs, planning retirement — based on guesses rather than facts. They accept salaries below market rates because they don't know what they're worth. They sign home loans they can't really afford. They under-save for retirement because the numbers feel abstract and distant.

We built CalcMyPay to change that. To put clear, accurate financial tools in everyone's hands, for free. To make the numbers that shape your life visible and understandable. To help you make financial decisions with confidence, not anxiety.

The problem we saw: South Africans face unique financial challenges — high inequality, complex tax rules, limited access to financial advice, and a legacy of financial exclusion. Generic international finance tools don't account for PAYE, UIF, medical aid tax credits, retirement annuities, or the Two-Pot system. South Africans deserve tools built specifically for their reality.

Our commitment to accuracy

Financial tools are only valuable if they're accurate. Wrong numbers lead to wrong decisions. That's why accuracy isn't just a feature for us — it's a non-negotiable principle that shapes everything we build.

How we ensure accuracy

  • Official SARS sources: Every tax calculation is built directly from official SARS tax tables, verified against SARS publications rather than secondary sources or outdated articles
  • Site-wide consistency: When tax brackets, rebates, or thresholds change, we update every affected calculator across the site — not just the flagship salary tool. Inconsistency between tools would undermine trust
  • Current tax year: We currently use SARS 2027 tax year tables (1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027) and update when new tables are released
  • Multiple verification: Calculations cross-checked against manual examples, SARS eFiling test cases, and professional tax practitioner review
  • Transparent methodology: Each calculator explains its assumptions and limitations — you know exactly what's included and what's not

Limitations we're transparent about

  • Estimates, not exact figures: Your actual take-home may vary slightly due to specific deductions, directives, or employer-specific arrangements
  • Standard assumptions: We use standard deduction patterns — unusual situations (multiple employers, complex fringe benefits) may need professional calculation
  • Not real-time: We update when SARS announces changes, not in real-time as your payroll processes
  • No individual circumstances: Tools can't account for your complete financial picture — that's what professional advisers are for

What CalcMyPay is not

Being clear about our limitations is as important as explaining our strengths. CalcMyPay has a specific role to play, and understanding that role helps you use the platform effectively.

We are not a registered financial adviser

CalcMyPay provides educational tools and information, not regulated financial advice under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services (FAIS) Act. Our calculators give you accurate estimates to inform your own decisions, but they cannot replace the personalised guidance of a registered FSCA financial adviser who understands your complete financial situation.

When to seek professional advice:

  • Complex tax situations (multiple income sources, foreign income, business ownership)
  • Investment decisions with significant capital
  • Estate planning and wealth structuring
  • Retirement planning with large portfolios
  • Divorce or business partnership financial splits
  • Cross-border financial matters

We are not affiliated with SARS or government

CalcMyPay is an independent platform. While we use official SARS tax tables for accuracy, we have no affiliation with SARS, the South African Revenue Service, the National Treasury, or any government department. We're a private educational resource.

We are not a financial product provider

We don't offer bank accounts, investments, insurance, loans, or any financial products. We don't partner with product providers for referrals or commissions. Our only role is to help you understand your numbers so you can make better decisions with whatever providers you choose.

We are not a substitute for professional advice

Our tools are designed to make you an informed participant in your financial decisions — someone who can have intelligent conversations with advisers, ask better questions, and verify that advice makes sense. They're not designed to replace advisers for complex situations.

The ideal use case: Use CalcMyPay to understand your situation, model scenarios, and prepare for conversations with professionals. Then take that understanding to a registered financial adviser for personalised advice on major decisions. This combination — informed client plus professional guidance — produces the best outcomes.

The numbers behind the numbers

Building accurate financial tools requires deep understanding of South African tax law, employment law, and financial regulations. Here's what goes into ensuring our tools work correctly:

Tax law expertise

  • Income Tax Act: Governs PAYE, rebates, deductions, and tax brackets
  • UIF Act: Defines unemployment insurance contributions and benefits
  • Skills Development Levies Act: SDL calculations for employers
  • Pension Funds Act: Retirement fund regulations and Two-Pot System
  • Medical Schemes Act: Medical aid regulations and tax credits

Employment law knowledge

  • Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA): Overtime rates, working hours, leave entitlements
  • Labour Relations Act: Employment contracts and conditions
  • Employment Equity Act: Understanding pay equity considerations

Financial regulations

  • FAIS Act: Understanding what constitutes financial advice vs information
  • FICA: Financial Intelligence Centre Act requirements
  • NCA (National Credit Act): Affordability assessments for credit
  • POPIA: Protection of Personal Information Act compliance

Our quality assurance process

Every calculator goes through rigorous testing before launch:

  1. Source verification: All calculations built from official SARS publications and relevant legislation
  2. Manual calculation check: Multiple test cases calculated manually and compared to tool outputs
  3. Edge case testing: Zero income, maximum thresholds, unusual deduction combinations
  4. Cross-calculator consistency: Verify that related calculators produce consistent results
  5. Professional review: Tax practitioners and financial advisers review complex calculations
  6. User feedback loop: Monitor user reports of discrepancies and investigate immediately
  7. Annual review: Complete review of all calculators against current tax tables and legislation

Guide

How to Live Within Your Means in South Africa

Read guide →

Why "living within your means" is harder to judge than it sounds

Most people have a rough sense of whether they are overspending, but a rough sense is not the same as knowing. Expenses creep — a subscription here, a bigger grocery basket there — until the gap between income and spending has quietly closed or gone negative. This tool forces the honest tally that intuition often avoids.

The categories that matter most

Not all spending is equal when it comes to financial health. Housing and transport are typically your two largest, least flexible costs — and the ones worth scrutinising hardest, because a small percentage change in either has an outsized effect on your monthly balance compared to trimming smaller discretionary items.

CategoryHealthy range (% of take-home)Watch for
Housing25-30%Above 35% leaves little room for anything else
Transport10-15%Car repayments plus fuel and insurance add up fast
Food10-15%Groceries plus eating out combined
Debt repaymentsUnder 20%Above this signals a debt problem, not just a budget one
Savings15-20%+The category most often sacrificed first — protect it

What a negative number really means

If your calculation shows you are spending more than you earn, this is the single most urgent financial signal to act on — more urgent than optimising investments or comparing interest rates. A structural monthly deficit, if unaddressed, is typically covered by growing debt, which compounds the problem every month it continues.

The order of operations when you find a gap

  • 1. Identify the biggest lever first — usually housing or transport, since cutting 10% there beats cutting 50% of a small expense category
  • 2. Separate needs from wants honestly — a gym membership and a streaming subscription are wants, however routine they feel
  • 3. Fix it before investing — there is no investment return that beats simply not running a deficit
  • 4. Rebuild your buffer — once balanced, prioritise an emergency fund before anything else

When your numbers look healthy

A positive gap is only the first checkpoint, not the finish line. The next question is whether that surplus is actually being saved and invested, or simply accumulating in a low-interest current account. Healthy spending habits combined with passive, unstructured saving still leave money underperforming — pair this result with our budget planner to give every rand of that surplus an actual job.

A quick sanity check before you commit to any change

Whatever change prompted you to run these numbers — a new rental, a car upgrade, a job offer — resist the urge to rely on a rough mental estimate. The categories that feel manageable individually often combine into a total that quietly exceeds what your income can sustainably support. Run the full picture through this tool before signing anything.

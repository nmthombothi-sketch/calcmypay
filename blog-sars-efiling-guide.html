SARS eFiling 2026: How to File Your Tax Return in South Africa

Complete step-by-step guide to filing your tax return on SARS eFiling, including auto-assessment, deadlines, required documents, refund timelines, verification processes, and dispute resolution.

Updated Aug 2026 16 min read SARS 2027 Tax Year

Filing your tax return on SARS eFiling sounds intimidating, but for most salary earners it takes about 30 minutes and can result in a welcome refund. The process has become significantly simpler in recent years, with SARS now pre-populating most of your return using data from employers, medical aids, and financial institutions. This comprehensive guide walks you through everything you need to know about filing your 2026 tax return: who must file, deadlines, required documents, step-by-step filing process, auto-assessments, refunds, verification, audits, and what to do if you disagree with your assessment.

Quick answer: Most salaried South Africans must file a tax return each year unless they earn under R500,000 from a single employer with no other income or deductions. Filing is free on SARS eFiling and may result in a refund if you have deductible expenses. Most refunds are paid within 72 hours if your return is straightforward and your banking details are verified.

Who Must File a Tax Return?

Understanding whether you need to file is the first step. The requirements differ based on your income sources and circumstances.

You MUST file if any of these apply:

Requirement Threshold/Condition Examples
Total income More than R500,000 per year Salary + bonus + investment income
Multiple income sources Income from more than one employer Two jobs, job change during year
Additional income Any rental, freelance, or business income Airbnb, consulting work, side hustle
Investment income Interest above R23,800 (under 65) or R34,500 (65+) Savings account, fixed deposits
Capital gains Any disposal of assets with gains Property sale, shares sale
Foreign income Any income from foreign sources Overseas employment, foreign dividends
Travel allowance Received any travel allowance Company travel allowance
Company car Received company car benefit Company vehicle for private use
Want to claim deductions Any deductions not on payroll RA contributions, medical expenses
SARS request SARS specifically asks you to file Letter or SMS from SARS

You may NOT need to file if:

  • You earned less than R500,000 from a single employer
  • You have no other income sources
  • You have no deductions to claim
  • You didn't receive a travel allowance or company car
  • All your tax was correctly deducted via PAYE
Important: Even if you're not required to file, it's often worth doing so anyway. You may be owed a refund for medical aid credits, RA contributions made privately, or other deductions your employer didn't account for. Filing is free and takes about 30 minutes.

2026 Filing Season Deadlines

SARS announces exact dates each year, but these are the typical timeframes for the 2026 filing season (for the 2026 tax year ending 28 February 2026):

Taxpayer Type Typical Opening Typical Deadline Notes
Auto-assessments July 2026 Various (check SMS/email) Must still review and accept/edit
Non-provisional (salary earners) July 2026 October 2026 Manual filing deadline
Provisional taxpayers July 2026 January 2027 Freelancers, business owners

Penalties for late filing

Missing the filing deadline has serious consequences:

  • Administrative penalty: R250 per month for each month the return is outstanding
  • Maximum penalty: R16,000 per outstanding return (64 months × R250)
  • Criminal prosecution: Possible for repeated non-compliance
  • Blocked refunds: SARS won't pay refunds if you have outstanding returns
  • Interest: Charged on any tax owed from the due date

Understanding Auto-Assessment

Auto-assessment is SARS's automated system that generates your tax return using data they already have from third parties. It's become increasingly common and simplifies the process significantly.

How auto-assessment works

  1. Data collection: SARS receives your IRP5 from your employer, medical aid certificates, investment income certificates from banks, retirement annuity certificates, etc.
  2. Automatic generation: SARS uses this data to pre-populate your ITR12 return
  3. Notification: You receive an SMS or email notifying you that your auto-assessment is ready
  4. Review required: You MUST log into eFiling to review the assessment
  5. Accept or edit: If correct, accept it. If not, edit and submit

What to check in your auto-assessment

Even though SARS pre-populates your return, you must verify:

  • Personal details: Name, ID number, address are correct
  • Employment income: Matches your IRP5 exactly
  • Medical aid: All contributions and dependants are captured
  • Investment income: All interest, dividends are included
  • Missing deductions: Add any RA contributions, medical expenses, travel claims not captured
  • Banking details: Correct for refund payment
Warning: Don't ignore an auto-assessment! You must either accept it or edit and submit it. Ignoring it can result in penalties, missed refunds, or SARS issuing an estimated assessment that may not be in your favor.

When auto-assessment is insufficient

You'll need to manually file (not just accept auto-assessment) if you have:

  • Complex deductions (home office, travel allowance claims)
  • Rental income or expenses
  • Capital gains or losses
  • Foreign income
  • Business or freelance income
  • Multiple employers not captured correctly
  • Any income or deductions not pre-populated

Documents You Need

Having all your documents ready before you start makes the filing process much smoother. Most are pre-loaded on eFiling, but you should have copies to verify accuracy.

Essential documents

Document Source What It Shows Pre-loaded?
IRP5/IT3(a) Employer Salary, PAYE, benefits, deductions Yes
Medical aid certificate Medical aid Contributions, credits, claims Yes
IT3(b) Banks, investment companies Interest earned Yes
IT3(c) Investment companies Dividends received Yes
IT3(s) Investment companies Capital gains/losses Yes
RA certificates RA providers Contributions made Yes
Travel logbook You (must maintain) Business vs private kilometers No
Medical expense receipts You (must keep) Out-of-pocket medical costs No
Section 18A certificates Charities Donations to PBOs No
Rental income records You (must keep) Rental received, expenses No
Freelance income records You (must keep) Invoices, payments received No

Record-keeping requirements

SARS requires you to keep all tax-related documents for at least 5 years from the date of submission. This includes:

  • All certificates (IRP5, IT3, RA, medical aid)
  • Bank statements
  • Receipts for deductions claimed
  • Travel logbooks
  • Rental agreements and expense receipts
  • Contracts and invoices for freelance work
  • Any correspondence with SARS

Keep both digital and physical copies. If SARS requests documents during verification or audit and you can't provide them, they may disallow your claims, resulting in additional tax, penalties, and interest.

Step-by-Step Filing Process

Here's the complete process for filing your tax return on SARS eFiling:

Step 1: Access eFiling

  • Go to www.sarsefiling.gov.za or use the SARS MobiApp
  • Log in with your username and password
  • If you don't have an account, click "Register" and follow the prompts

Step 2: Open your tax return

  • Click on "Returns" in the main menu
  • Select "Returns Issued"
  • Find your ITR12 for the 2026 tax year
  • Click "Open" to start the return

Step 3: Review pre-populated information

SARS will have already filled in most sections using data from third parties:

  • Employment income: From your IRP5
  • Medical aid: Contributions and credits
  • Investment income: Interest, dividends from banks
  • Retirement contributions: RA, pension, provident fund

Carefully review each section to ensure accuracy. Compare against your documents.

Step 4: Add missing information

Add any income or deductions not pre-populated:

Rental income

  • Navigate to "Other Income" section
  • Select "Rental Income"
  • Enter total rental received
  • Enter allowable expenses (rates, maintenance, interest, etc.)

Travel allowance claims

  • Navigate to "Travel Allowance" section
  • Enter your logbook details
  • Calculate business kilometers vs total kilometers
  • Claim based on actual costs or SARS fixed rates

Medical expenses

  • Navigate to "Medical Expenses" section
  • Enter out-of-pocket medical expenses (not covered by medical aid)
  • SARS calculates the allowable deduction (expenses exceeding 7.5% of income)

Retirement annuity contributions

  • Navigate to "Retirement Fund Contributions" section
  • Enter RA contributions made privately (not through employer)
  • SARS calculates the deduction (up to 27.5% of income or R430,000)

Donations

  • Navigate to "Donations" section
  • Enter donations to approved PBOs (with Section 18A certificates)
  • Deduction limited to 10% of taxable income

Step 5: Review the calculation

Before submitting:

  • Click "Calculate" to see your tax liability or refund
  • Review the summary showing:
    • Total income
    • Total deductions
    • Taxable income
    • Tax calculated
    • Rebates and credits applied
    • Final tax payable or refund due
  • If you owe tax, you'll need to pay by the deadline
  • If you're due a refund, ensure your banking details are correct

Step 6: Submit your return

  • Click "Submit" to file your return
  • You'll receive an ITA34 assessment confirming submission
  • Save or print this assessment for your records
  • SARS will notify you if your return is selected for verification

Getting Your Tax Refund

If you've overpaid tax during the year (common if you have deductions not accounted for in monthly PAYE), SARS will refund the difference.

Refund timeline

Scenario Typical Timeline Conditions
Straightforward return 72 hours (3 business days) No verification, banking details verified
Selected for verification 21 business days After submitting requested documents
Selected for audit 3-6 months Comprehensive review process
Outstanding returns Blocked until filed All returns must be up to date
Owing SARS from previous years Offset against debt Refund used to settle outstanding tax

Ensuring fast refund payment

To get your refund as quickly as possible:

  • Verify banking details: Ensure your bank account details are correct and verified on eFiling
  • File accurately: Double-check all information before submitting
  • Have documents ready: If selected for verification, submit requested documents immediately
  • Clear outstanding returns: File any overdue returns from previous years
  • Pay outstanding tax: Settle any amounts owed to SARS

Common reasons for refunds

The most common reasons people receive refunds:

  • RA contributions: Made privately, not through employer payroll
  • Medical expenses: Out-of-pocket costs not covered by medical aid
  • Travel allowance: Business use exceeds the 80% default inclusion
  • Medical aid credits: Not fully reflected in monthly PAYE
  • Multiple employers: Each employer applies full tax threshold
  • Part-year employment: Worked only part of the tax year
Pro tip: The most common reason for refunds is retirement annuity contributions made outside your employer's payroll. If you contribute to an RA privately, you almost certainly have a refund waiting. Make sure you claim it by filing your return and including your RA certificate.

SARS Verification Process

Verification is a routine check where SARS asks you to provide supporting documents for claims you made. About 20-30% of returns are selected for verification — it doesn't mean you did anything wrong.

What happens during verification

  1. Notification: You receive an email or SMS that your return is selected for verification
  2. Letter via eFiling: Detailed letter explaining which documents are required
  3. 21 business days: You have 21 business days to submit requested documents
  4. Upload documents: Upload via eFiling or submit at SARS branch
  5. Review: SARS reviews your documents (can take several weeks)
  6. Outcome: SARS either accepts your claims or makes adjustments
  7. Refund release: If satisfied, SARS releases your refund

Common verification requests

Claim Type Documents Requested
Medical expenses Receipts, invoices, proof of payment
Travel allowance Logbook, vehicle registration, fuel receipts
Home office Floor plan, employment contract, utility bills
Donations Section 18A certificates from PBOs
Rental income Rental agreements, bank statements, expense receipts
Retirement contributions RA certificates, proof of payment

Tips for successful verification

  • Respond promptly: Submit documents within the 21-day deadline
  • Provide complete documentation: Don't submit partial records
  • Keep copies: Always keep copies of everything you submit
  • Be organized: Label documents clearly and submit in logical order
  • Follow up: If you haven't heard back after 4-6 weeks, contact SARS

SARS Audit Process

An audit is more comprehensive and serious than verification. It involves detailed examination of your financial affairs and can take several months.

What triggers an audit

Common audit triggers include:

  • High-risk claims: Home office expenses, large travel claims
  • Unusual patterns: Deductions unusually high relative to income
  • Inconsistencies: Discrepancies between your return and third-party data
  • Random selection: SARS audits a percentage of all returns
  • Previous issues: History of non-compliance or disputes
  • Complex affairs: Multiple income sources, business income, foreign income
  • Large transactions: Significant capital gains, property sales

The audit process

  1. Notification: Letter informing you of audit selection
  2. Scope definition: Letter detailing what will be audited
  3. Document request: Comprehensive list of required documents
  4. Submission: You provide requested documentation
  5. Review: SARS examines documents (may request additional information)
  6. Field audit (if needed): SARS officials may visit your premises
  7. Draft findings: SARS shares preliminary findings
  8. Response: You can respond to draft findings
  9. Final assessment: SARS issues final assessment with any adjustments

Audit timeline

Typical audit timelines:

  • Simple audit: 3-6 months
  • Complex audit: 6-12 months
  • Very complex (business, multiple entities): 12-24 months

Your rights during an audit

You have specific rights during a SARS audit:

  • Professional treatment: SARS officials must be professional and courteous
  • Clear communication: You must be informed of audit scope and progress
  • Representation: You can appoint a tax practitioner to represent you
  • Reasonable timeframes: Audit must be completed within reasonable time
  • Confidentiality: Your information must be kept confidential
  • Dispute rights: You can dispute any assessment resulting from the audit

Objecting to a SARS Assessment

If you disagree with your assessment (whether from your own return or a SARS audit), you have the right to object.

When to object

You should object if:

  • SARS disallowed legitimate deductions
  • SARS made calculation errors
  • SARS included income you didn't earn
  • SARS applied incorrect tax rates
  • You have evidence that contradicts SARS's findings

How to object

  1. Time limit: Object within 30 business days of receiving the assessment
  2. Log into eFiling: Go to "Disputes" section
  3. Select "Notice of Objection": Choose the assessment you're objecting to
  4. Provide reasons: Clearly explain why you disagree, with specific details
  5. Attach evidence: Upload supporting documents
  6. Submit: Submit your objection

SARS response timeline

  • Request for documents: SARS may request additional information (you have 30 days to respond)
  • Decision: SARS must respond within 60 business days of receiving your objection
  • Outcome: SARS either allows your objection (amends assessment) or disallows it

If SARS disallows your objection

You have further appeal rights:

  1. Appeal: File a notice of appeal within 30 business days
  2. Alternative dispute resolution: SARS may offer mediation
  3. Tax Board: For disputes under R1 million, you can approach the Tax Board
  4. Tax Court: For larger disputes or if Tax Board decision is unfavorable
Important: Always keep detailed records of all correspondence with SARS, including dates, reference numbers, and copies of all documents submitted. This is crucial if you need to appeal or take the matter to court.

Common Deductions to Claim

Understanding which deductions you can claim helps ensure you don't miss out on legitimate tax savings.

Retirement annuity contributions

  • Limit: Up to 27.5% of taxable income or R430,000 (whichever is lower)
  • Requirement: RA certificate from provider
  • Common scenario: Contributions made privately (not through employer)
  • Impact: Significant tax savings, especially for high earners

Medical expenses

  • Qualifying: Out-of-pocket expenses not covered by medical aid
  • Threshold: Expenses must exceed 7.5% of taxable income
  • Examples: Specialist consultations, medication, dental work, optical
  • Requirement: Receipts and proof of payment

Travel allowance claims

  • Requirement: Must have received travel allowance and kept logbook
  • Calculation: Based on actual business kilometers vs total kilometers
  • Methods: Actual costs (fuel, maintenance, insurance) or SARS fixed rates
  • Common benefit: Can significantly reduce tax on travel allowance

Home office expenses

  • Requirements: Dedicated workspace, used regularly and exclusively for work, more than 50% of duties performed there
  • Deductible: Proportionate share of rent/bond interest, rates, electricity, internet, cleaning
  • Calculation: Based on floor area of office vs total home
  • Warning: Frequently audited — ensure you meet all requirements

Donations to PBOs

  • Limit: Up to 10% of taxable income
  • Requirement: Section 18A certificate from approved Public Benefit Organization
  • Examples: Charities, educational institutions, religious organizations
  • Keep: All Section 18A certificates as proof

Wear and tear on work equipment

  • Qualifying: Equipment used for work (laptop, phone, tools)
  • Calculation: Based on SARS depreciation tables
  • Requirement: Proof of purchase, must be used for work purposes
  • Common items: Computers, cellphones, office furniture

Provisional Tax Requirements

Provisional taxpayers have additional obligations beyond the annual return.

Who is a provisional taxpayer?

  • Freelancers and independent contractors
  • Business owners (sole proprietors, partnerships)
  • People with significant rental income
  • People with substantial investment income
  • Directors of private companies (with some exceptions)

Provisional tax obligations

Payment Due Date Calculation
First period 31 August 50% of estimated annual tax
Second period 28/29 February 100% of estimated annual tax (less first payment)
Voluntary third period 30 September Top-up if you underestimated

Penalties for provisional tax non-compliance

  • Late payment penalty: 10% of amount not paid
  • Underestimation penalty: Up to 20% if estimate is less than 80% of actual taxable income
  • Interest: Charged on late payments from due date

Common Filing Mistakes to Avoid

Avoiding these common mistakes ensures smooth processing and faster refunds.

Mistake 1: Ignoring auto-assessments

The mistake: Not reviewing or accepting/editing auto-assessments

The consequence: Penalties, missed refunds, or SARS issuing unfavorable estimated assessments

The fix: Always log in and review auto-assessments, even if you think they're correct

Mistake 2: Incorrect banking details

The mistake: Banking details not verified or incorrect

The consequence: Refund delayed or paid to wrong account

The fix: Verify banking details on eFiling before submitting return

Mistake 3: Missing income sources

The mistake: Not declaring all income (freelance, rental, interest)

The consequence: Penalties, interest, potential criminal prosecution

The fix: Declare ALL income sources, even small amounts

Mistake 4: Claiming without documentation

The mistake: Claiming deductions without supporting documents

The consequence: Claims disallowed during verification, penalties

The fix: Only claim deductions you can prove with documentation

Mistake 5: Travel allowance without logbook

The mistake: Claiming travel expenses without a proper logbook

The consequence: SARS disallows claim, applies default 80% inclusion

The fix: Keep a contemporaneous logbook with date, odometer, destination, purpose

Mistake 6: Home office without meeting requirements

The mistake: Claiming home office when requirements aren't met

The consequence: Claim disallowed, potential penalties (frequently audited)

The fix: Only claim if you have a dedicated space used exclusively and regularly for work

Mistake 7: Missing deadlines

The mistake: Filing after the deadline

The consequence: Administrative penalties of R250 per month

The fix: File before the deadline, request extension if needed

Mistake 8: Not keeping records

The mistake: Not keeping supporting documents for 5 years

The consequence: Can't prove claims during verification or audit

The fix: Keep all tax documents for at least 5 years (digital and physical copies)

Mistake 9: Ignoring SARS correspondence

The mistake: Not responding to verification requests or letters

The consequence: Claims disallowed, additional assessments, penalties

The fix: Respond to all SARS correspondence within specified timeframes

Mistake 10: Not reviewing pre-populated data

The mistake: Blindly accepting pre-populated information

The consequence: Missing deductions or incorrect information

The fix: Carefully review all pre-populated data against your documents

Frequently Asked Questions

Who must file a tax return in South Africa in 2026?

You must file a tax return if: you earned more than R500,000 in total, had income from more than one employer, earned additional income (rental, freelance, interest above R23,800 exemption), want to claim deductions (retirement annuity, medical expenses, travel allowance), received a travel allowance or company car benefit, conducted any business or trade, or if SARS specifically requested you to file. If none of these apply and you only earn salary from one employer under R500,000, you may not need to file, but it's often worth doing to claim refunds.

What is SARS auto-assessment and do I need to file if I receive one?

Auto-assessment is when SARS automatically generates your tax return using data they already have (IRP5 from employer, medical aid certificates, investment income). You receive it via SMS or email. You MUST still log into eFiling to review it — check all information is correct and add any missing deductions (RA contributions, medical expenses, travel claims). If everything is correct, simply accept it. If not, edit and submit. Ignoring an auto-assessment can result in penalties or missed refunds.

How long does a SARS tax refund take?

SARS refunds are typically paid into your verified bank account within 72 hours (3 business days) of a successful assessment, provided: your banking details are verified, you have no outstanding tax returns, you don't owe SARS money from previous years, and your return isn't selected for verification or audit. If selected for verification, expect 21 business days. If audited, it can take 3-6 months. The fastest way to get your refund is to file accurately the first time with all supporting documents ready.

What documents do I need to file my tax return?

Essential documents: IRP5/IT3(a) from your employer, medical aid tax certificate, retirement annuity certificates, IT3(b) interest certificates from banks, IT3(c) dividend certificates, travel logbook (if you have a travel allowance), rental income records, freelance income records. Most of these are pre-loaded on eFiling by SARS, but you should have copies to verify accuracy. Keep all documents for at least 5 years as SARS can request them during verification or audit.

What are the tax filing deadlines for 2026?

For the 2026 filing season (2026 tax year ending 28 February 2026): Auto-assessments typically open in July 2026, non-provisional taxpayers (salary earners) manual filing deadline is usually October 2026, and provisional taxpayers (freelancers, business owners) must file by January 2027. SARS announces exact dates annually. Missing deadlines results in administrative penalties of R250 per month (up to R16,000 per return) plus potential criminal prosecution for repeated non-compliance.

What is the difference between SARS verification and audit?

Verification is a routine check where SARS asks you to upload supporting documents for claims you made (usually within 21 business days). It's common and doesn't mean you did anything wrong — about 20-30% of returns are verified. An audit is more serious and comprehensive, involving detailed examination of your finances, potentially requesting bank statements, contracts, and extensive documentation. Audits can take 3-6 months and may involve SARS officials contacting third parties. Both can delay your refund until resolved.

How do I object to a SARS assessment I disagree with?

You can object via eFiling within 30 business days of receiving the assessment (ITA34). Steps: 1) Log into eFiling, 2) Select 'Dispute Resolution', 3) Choose 'Notice of Objection', 4) Provide detailed reasons with supporting evidence, 5) Submit. SARS must respond within 60 business days. If they disallow your objection, you can appeal within 30 days, and if still unsuccessful, take the matter to the Tax Board (for disputes under R1 million) or Tax Court. Always keep copies of all correspondence and evidence.

What deductions can I claim on my tax return?

Common deductions include: Retirement annuity contributions (up to 27.5% of income or R430,000), medical expenses not covered by medical aid (exceeding 7.5% of income), travel expenses against travel allowance (with logbook), home office expenses (if you have a dedicated workspace used more than 50% for work), donations to approved PBOs (up to 10% of income with Section 18A certificates), wear-and-tear on work equipment, and certain business expenses for freelancers. Always keep supporting documentation for 5 years.

What triggers a SARS audit or verification?

Common triggers include: unusually high deductions relative to income, claims for home office expenses (frequently audited), large retirement annuity contributions, travel allowance claims without proper logbooks, rental income declarations, significant capital gains, inconsistencies between your return and third-party data (employer IRP5, bank IT3s), random selection (SARS audits a percentage of all returns), and previous compliance issues. However, verification is routine and doesn't indicate wrongdoing — it's simply SARS confirming your claims.

How long must I keep my tax documents?

SARS requires you to keep all tax-related documents for at least 5 years from the date of submission. This includes IRP5s, medical aid certificates, RA certificates, bank statements, logbooks, receipts for deductions, rental agreements, and any correspondence with SARS. Keep both digital and physical copies. If SARS requests documents during verification or audit and you can't provide them, they may disallow your claims, resulting in additional tax, penalties, and interest. For property sales, keep records for the entire ownership period plus 5 years.
